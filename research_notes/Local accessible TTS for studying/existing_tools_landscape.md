# Existing Read-Aloud / TTS Tools Landscape (accessibility, ADHD, autism, dyslexia), as of 2026

Research date: 2026-10-25. Each claim has a source. Many pricing figures come from aggregator sites, and those are marked as such. Items I could not source are listed under Gaps and not stated as fact.

## Q1. Commercial tools: features, pricing, free-tier limits

### Takeaway
Commercial tools split into two groups. Consumer apps (Speechify, NaturalReader, ElevenReader, Voice Dream) sell natural voices on a subscription and limit the free tier by voice quality, speed, or hours. Literacy suites (Read&Write, Immersive Reader, Helperbird, BeeLine) add dyslexia supports such as line focus, masking, fonts and colour gradients. None of the sources I found say any of them skip PDF headers, footers or citations well.

### Cited Findings
**Speechify**
- Premium costs $29/month or $139/year (about $11.58/month). One guide says these figures were checked against Speechify's pricing page in Aug 2026. — [casrai.org guide](https://casrai.org/guides/speechify-pricing); [CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/ai-voice-tools/speechify)
- Free plan: about 10 basic voices and a 1.5x speed cap. Premium adds 200+ natural voices, 60+ languages, up to 5x speed, offline downloads and AI features. CostBench also lists a 5-file library limit on the free plan. These figures are from an aggregator. — [CostBench free plan](https://costbench.com/software/ai-voice-tools/speechify/free-plan/)
- Users on Reddit and the BBB report being auto-charged for a full year after a free trial. These are user reports, not verified company data. — [CostBench hidden costs](https://www.costbench.com/software/ai-voice-tools/speechify/hidden-costs/)
- An app-store review analysis lists cost as a significant concern "especially students", plus trouble cancelling and unexpected charges. — [Kimola report](https://kimola.com/reports/exclusive-insight-speechify-user-feedback-analysis-report-app-store-us-156083)
- A student reviewer said that for e-textbook readings "you have to scan the individual pages into Speechify, which is incredibly time consuming". Another review is titled "Buggy, distracting, waste of time". — [JustUseApp reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1209815023/speechify-audio-text-reader/reviews)
- The best voices and features are paid-only. Speechify is marketed to people with ADHD and dyslexia. — [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/what-is-speechify-everything-we-know-about-the-ai-text-to-speech-tool)

**ElevenReader (ElevenLabs)**
- Free: 10 hours of text-to-audio per month, resetting monthly. Ultra: $11/month, or $99/year (about $8.25/month). Hour packs can be bought without a subscription. — [ElevenReader pricing](https://www.elevenreader.io/pricing); [ElevenLabs help: hours](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/product/mobile-apps/eleven-reader/how-do-eleven-reader-hours-work)
- Free users cannot download audio for offline use. Ultra is "unlimited" but has a daily audio cap. — [ElevenReader pricing](https://www.elevenreader.io/pricing)

**NaturalReader**
- Free plan: unlimited listening with basic voices, but daily caps on AI voices. Sources disagree on the cap: one says 20 min/day for Premium voices and 5 min/day for Plus voices, another says 5 min/day for Plus voices. No MP3 download on the free plan. — [videosdk review](https://www.videosdk.live/developer-hub/tts/naturalreader-text-to-speech-review); [ElevenReader blog (competitor)](https://elevenreader.io/blog/naturalreader-alternatives); [smallest.ai](https://smallest.ai/blog/naturalreader-review-best-alternatives-for-developers-and-creators-in-2026)
- Paid prices conflict between sources, ranging from about $9.99/month for Premium to $19–25.90/month for Plus or Pro. Commercial use needs a separate licence. — [videosdk review](https://www.videosdk.live/developer-hub/tts/naturalreader-text-to-speech-review); [TechRadar review](https://www.techradar.com/au/reviews/natural-reader)

**Voice Dream Reader**
- In 2023 the app was bought by Applause Group. In 2024 it switched to a subscription of $79/year ($59 intro), and existing buyers would have lost the ability to add new content. After a backlash, Applause reversed this for existing purchasers. — [Perkins](https://www.perkins.org/resource/voice-dream-reader-subscription-controversy/); [iMore](https://imore.com/apps/another-app-subscription-model-switcheroo-has-users-up-in-arms-and-this-time-they-probably-have-a-reason-to-be-annoyed); [Six Colors](https://sixcolors.com/link/2024/04/another-app-switches-to-a-subscription-model); [AppleVis](https://applevis.com/comment/165340)
- The current price for new users is about $80/year according to one source of uncertain date. — [Perkins](https://www.perkins.org/resource/voice-dream-reader-subscription-controversy/)

**Read&Write (Texthelp / Everway)**
- Features: TTS, word prediction, a dictionary and picture dictionary, translation and screen masking. Runs as a browser extension and desktop app across Google Docs, Word, PDFs and the web. — [EdTech Impact](https://edtechimpact.com/products/texthelp)
- Sold per seat to institutions. In the UK, school licences start at £1,250 and an individual school licence is £250+VAT. It is free for K-12 teachers. — [Everway pricing](https://www.everway.com/en-gb/products/read-and-write-education/pricing); [Everway US](https://www.everway.com/products/read-and-write-education/pricing/)

**Microsoft Immersive Reader**
- Line Focus shows 1, 3 or 5 lines at a time. — [Microsoft: Immersive Reader for OneNote](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/education/use-immersive-reader-for-onenote)
- Picture Dictionary: select a word to hear it and see an image of it. — [Microsoft OneNote IR](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/education/use-immersive-reader-for-onenote)
- Parts-of-speech colouring and labels for nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs. Read Aloud highlights each word as it speaks. — [Bradford help sheet](https://bso.bradford.gov.uk/userfiles/file/!Learning%20Support/Help%20Sheet%20-%20Microsoft%20Immersive%20Reader%20and%20Dictate.pdf); [SUNY Empire KB](https://help.sunyempire.edu/TDClient/277/Portal/KB/Article/11238/Microsoft-Immersive-Reader)
- Which features are available depends on the app. Some, such as Picture Dictionary, are web-only. — [Bradford help sheet](https://bso.bradford.gov.uk/userfiles/file/!Learning%20Support/Help%20Sheet%20-%20Microsoft%20Immersive%20Reader%20and%20Dictate.pdf)

**Helperbird**
- A browser extension with TTS, dyslexia fonts, a reading ruler, word spacing and an Immersive Reader mode. Line spacing, line width, columns and letter spacing are Pro-only. — [Helperbird pricing](https://helperbird.com/pricing/)
- Price conflict: the pricing page and Firefox listing say Pro is $30/year, while a help article says $6.99/month, $60/year, or $40/year for students. — [Helperbird pricing](https://helperbird.com/pricing/); [Helperbird help](https://www.helperbird.com/help/what-is-helperbird-pro)

**BeeLine Reader** (colour-gradient line guidance)
- WestEd evaluation (2018, 530 seventh graders): higher engagement and preference, and measured comprehension gains. BeeLine did not fund it. — [RIF/WestEd PDF](https://www.rif.org/sites/default/files/documents/2019/10/24/Support_Materials/BeeLineWestEdStudyFinal.pdf)
- Leiden study (2022): helped only some beginning readers in some situations. The authors urge formal testing of vendor claims. — [Leiden University](https://universiteitleiden.nl/en/research/research-output/social-and-behavioural-sciences/digital-reading-applications-bring-color-to-reading); [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/b48617b6416f4030ae9bf57ab79d1318)
- At the time of writing the method had not been independently peer-reviewed, and a placebo effect is possible. — [Behavioral Scientist](https://behavioralscientist.org/speed-reading-apps-open-research-questions/)

### Inferences
- Free tiers are limited in three ways, and these are the main pain points a free local tool can remove: voice-quality time caps (NaturalReader), hour caps (ElevenReader 10 h/month), and speed or library caps (Speechify 1.5x, 5 files). For a student with long PDF notes, 10 h/month runs out quickly.
- Subscription distrust has evidence behind it: the Speechify auto-renew reports and the Voice Dream backlash. "One-time / free / no account" is a real selling point.
- The literacy suites have the strongest visual supports (line focus, masking, colour gradients). The consumer apps have the best voices. Few tools combine natural voices with strong visual focus aids for free.

### Gaps
- I did not research Kurzweil 3000, Claro, Apple Spoken Content, or Google Chrome reading mode / Read Along directly in this session (tool budget). From memory, not sourced here: Chrome's Reading Mode has had read aloud with word highlighting since 2024, and Apple Spoken Content offers Speak Screen and Highlight Content. Verify both before using them.
- BeeLine pricing was not found.
- I found no product page that documents "skip headers/footers/citations" rules or sleep timers in these products. That needs a feature-page check.

## Q2. Open-source and free tools; which use Kokoro or Piper locally

### Takeaway
Several open-source projects now pair local neural voices (mainly Kokoro-82M, sometimes Piper) with ebook or PDF reading. OpenReader WebUI is the closest open-source "Speechify" with read-along highlighting. Most of the others (audiblez, epub_to_audiobook) are batch audiobook converters rather than interactive study readers.

### Cited Findings
- **OpenReader WebUI** (richardr1126, MIT): reads EPUB, PDF, DOCX, TXT and Markdown with read-along highlighting. Uses Kokoro-FastAPI through an OpenAI-compatible endpoint (Docker). Optional word-level highlighting via whisper.cpp timestamps. Exports M4B/MP3. Sources disagree on the version (v3.0 in May 2026 vs v4.4.0) and on the author's name. — [newreleases.io](https://newreleases.io/project/github/richardr1126/openreader/release/v1.0.0); [noted.lol](https://noted.lol/openreader-webui/); [creativeainews](https://www.creativeainews.com/blog/openreader-v3-open-source-tts-2026/); [selfhostyourself](https://selfhostyourself.com/services/openreader-webui)
- **OpenWebTTS**: an MIT, self-hostable "open source Speechify alternative" for PDF/EPUB with local models. Small project (about 43 stars). — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/openwebtts/about)
- **Yapit**: an open-source TTS platform for documents and research papers that runs online, in Docker, or self-hosted. — [AlternativeTo Speechify alternatives](https://www.alternativeto.net/software/speechify/)
- **audiblez** (MIT): converts EPUB to M4B with Kokoro-82M locally. Has a CLI and GUI and needs ffmpeg and espeak-ng. A roughly 160k-character book takes about 5 min on a T4 GPU and about 1 h on an M2 CPU. — [codeyu/audiblez](https://github.com/codeyu/audiblez); [dsebastien notes](https://notes.dsebastien.net/30+Areas/33+Permanent+notes/33.02+Content/Audiblez)
- **epub_to_audiobook** (p0n1): supports Piper, Kokoro, EdgeTTS, Azure and OpenAI. Has a CLI and Gradio UI, chapter metadata and Docker. — [docsearch mirror of repo](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/p0n1/epub_to_audiobook)
- **Kokoro-82M**: an Apache-licensed open-weight model with 82M parameters, trained on under 100 h of audio. — [codeyu/audiblez](https://github.com/codeyu/audiblez); [AlternativeTo Kokoro](https://alternativeto.net/software/kokoro-82m/?p=2)
- **Readest** (AGPL-3.0; a Foliate rewrite on Tauri/Next.js; Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS and web): read aloud with native, browser or cloud voices and sentence highlighting, a customizable highlight style (v0.9.91), bookmarks, notes, and highlight export. — [readest.com llms.txt](https://readest.com/llms.txt); [Readest docs](https://readest.com/docs); [linuxlinks](https://www.linuxlinks.com/readest-modern-feature-rich-ebook-reader)
- **Thorium Reader** (EDRLab): read aloud with highlighting uses the OS voices, but there is **no read aloud for PDF**. It favours EPUB 3. — [Thorium FAQ](https://thorium.edrlab.org/en/th3/400_ressources/430_faq); [Thorium accessibility](https://thorium.edrlab.org/en/docs/300_accessibility/_print/)
- **Read Aloud extension** (open source on GitHub): uses browser-native voices plus cloud voices (Google Wavenet, Polly, Watson, Microsoft), some of them paid. Reads PDFs, Google Docs and Kindle, with highlighting options. I found no confirmation of Piper or Kokoro support. — [Chrome Web Store](https://chrome.google.com/webstore/detail/read-aloud-a-text-to-spee/hdhinadidafjejdhmfkjgnolgimiaplp?hl=pl); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/read-aloud)
- **Balabolka** (free, Windows): uses any installed SAPI voice, has regex pronunciation rules, and exports MP3/WAV/OGG/M4A. Its PDF extraction discards layout, there is no OCR, and it works well with NVDA. — [Audeus Balabolka alternatives](https://www.audeus.com/product-reviews/balabolka-alternatives); [EMU screen reader guide](https://www.emich.edu/drc/documents/screen_reader.pdf)

### Inferences
- The Kokoro stack (Kokoro-FastAPI, whisper alignment for word timing) is the de facto local engine for open-source Speechify clones in 2025–26. Piper shows up mainly in batch converters.
- Gap in the open-source space: the PDF-aware tools (OpenReader) are general-purpose and need Docker. Thorium skips PDF read aloud entirely. Balabolka's PDF extraction is crude. I found no project focused on cleaning study-note PDFs (stripping headers, footers and page numbers, joining hyphens, handling citations) combined with ADHD-friendly playback.

### Gaps
- ebook2audiobook (DrewThomasson), NVDA PDF behaviour, Calibre TTS (built into the Calibre viewer since about v6.x; unverified here) and "open-speech" were not confirmed in this session.
- Read Aloud extension support for Piper (e.g. via a "Piper TTS" companion extension) is unverified.

## Q3. Why Edge Read Aloud (and similar) performs poorly on PDFs

### Takeaway
Edge reads the PDF's raw text layer in the order it was painted on the page. Most study-note PDFs are untagged, so line breaks become pauses, and running headers, footers and page numbers are read as body text. Users have reported this since 2020–21, and I found no Edge setting that fixes it.

### Cited Findings
- Edge Read Aloud reads PDF headers and footers, including page numbers, and "there doesn't seem to be any way" to skip them. The suggested workaround was opening the file in Word. — [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/a/2464196); [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/a/2453451)
- Text structure such as line breaks and superscripts makes the reader insert artificial pauses or misread text (Edge Insider thread, 2020). I could not fetch the page directly (DNS error). The claim is from the search summary. — [Tech Community thread](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/discussions/edgeinsiderdiscussions/pdf--read-aloud-natural-voices-issues/1546437)
- A 2021 report says Edge stopped mid-paragraph at ellipses and exclamation marks, so the user had to press "next paragraph". — [Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/a/2453451)
- Adobe Acrobat Read Out Loud has the same complaint about pausing between lines (2018). — [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/acrobat-discussions/read-aloud/m-p/10179100/highlight/true)
- Readlang users work around the same problem by stripping single line breaks before listening. — [Readlang forum](https://forum.readlang.com/t/avoiding-line-breaks/4637)
- Zotero users report similar problems in their PDF read-aloud feedback thread. — [Zotero forums](https://forums.zotero.org/discussion/comment/509187/)
- Root cause: an untagged PDF has no structure, so assistive technology can't identify headings, paragraphs or reading order. Content may be read wrongly or skipped. — [Continual Engine](https://www.continualengine.com/blog/common-pdf-accessibility-errors-to-avoid/)
- A PDF's paint order need not match the logical reading order. PDF/UA (ISO 14289-1) requires a structure tree with page artifacts (headers, footers, page numbers) marked so they can be skipped. — [losLab PDFium article](https://blog.loslab.com/en-us/pdfium-component/pdfium-component-accessible-reader-delphi.html)
- Running headers and footers should be tagged as artifacts so screen readers skip them. Doing this means manually tagging in Acrobat. — [TCEQ artifacts tutorial](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/downloads/agency/accessibility/tutorials/2-10-designating-pdf-artifacts); [accessABILITY RGD](https://accessability.rgd.ca/digital-media/accessible-pdfs/)

### Inferences
- A local tool can't rely on PDF tags, because student notes exported from Word, Notion or slides are rarely tagged properly. It has to rebuild the reading order itself:
  1. join lines into paragraphs and de-hyphenate line-end splits
  2. detect repeated top and bottom lines across pages and drop them as headers, footers or page numbers
  3. handle superscripts, footnotes and citations, e.g. "[12]" or "(Smith, 2020)"
  4. detect multi-column layouts
  5. flag tables and math for skipping or a summary
- Multi-column reading order is a likely failure point too. OpenReader uses alignment for multi-column PDFs, which suggests it's a known problem. — [sourcepulse OpenReader](https://www.sourcepulse.org/projects/21284509)

### Gaps
- I found no source specifically about hyphenation, math symbols or tables in Edge Read Aloud. These are likely problems but undocumented in what I found.
- I found no current (2025–26) official Microsoft documentation, and the forum reports are from 2020–21. Behaviour may have changed.

## Q4. Common user complaints about TTS study tools (Reddit and reviews)

### Takeaway
The documented complaints are cost and subscriptions (especially for students), aggressive billing and hard cancellation, paywalled natural voices, time and hour caps, and friction getting textbook content in. I could not retrieve Reddit threads directly. Complaints about voice fatigue, robotic voices and privacy are therefore inferred rather than sourced.

### Cited Findings
- Cost is a significant concern "especially students and those on a budget". Users report difficulties cancelling and unexpected charges, which the source estimates (not measures) at about 20% of users. — [Kimola](https://kimola.com/reports/exclusive-insight-speechify-user-feedback-analysis-report-app-store-us-156083)
- Users report being auto-charged for a full year after a trial (Reddit and BBB reports, via aggregator). — [CostBench](https://www.costbench.com/software/ai-voice-tools/speechify/hidden-costs/)
- Getting content in is slow: e-textbook pages have to be scanned one at a time. The app pushes premium trials. — [JustUseApp](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1209815023/speechify-audio-text-reader/reviews)
- Sentiment split from one aggregator: 58.8% positive, 41.2% negative. — [onclarity](https://www.onclarity.com/leaderboard/insight/speechify)
- Paying again for features already bought (Voice Dream) drew strong backlash from blind and print-disabled users. — [AppleVis](https://applevis.com/comment/165340); [Perkins](https://www.perkins.org/resource/voice-dream-reader-subscription-controversy/)
- Daily caps on good voices: NaturalReader allows about 5–20 min/day of AI voices on the free plan. — [videosdk](https://www.videosdk.live/developer-hub/tts/naturalreader-text-to-speech-review)

### Inferences
- Robotic free voices (basic voices on free tiers) and premium voices behind caps push users either to pay or to put up with fatigue. A local Kokoro voice removes that trade-off.
- Uploading course notes to cloud services (Speechify, ElevenReader) raises privacy concerns. A local tool avoids the issue by design. This is an inference; I found no sourced complaint.

### Gaps
- I retrieved no direct Reddit threads (r/ADHD, r/dyslexia, r/autism, r/college). The search returned only aggregators. A follow-up should search reddit.com directly for "Speechify alternative", "text to speech ADHD study" and "Edge read aloud PDF".
- Voice fatigue, robotic-voice and privacy complaints are not sourced in these notes.

## Q5. Feature ideas for ADHD and autistic users

### Takeaway
The features that recur across the established tools are synchronized word or sentence highlighting (bimodal reading), line focus and masking, adjustable speed, bookmarks and notes, and colour or font customization. What I found nothing on is predictability-oriented design for autistic users: no autoplay surprises, a consistent voice, replaying the last sentence, and clear progress through a long document.

### Cited Findings
- Word highlighting during read aloud and line focus (1/3/5 lines) are standard in Immersive Reader. — [Microsoft](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/education/use-immersive-reader-for-onenote); [Bradford](https://bso.bradford.gov.uk/userfiles/file/!Learning%20Support/Help%20Sheet%20-%20Microsoft%20Immersive%20Reader%20and%20Dictate.pdf)
- Sentence highlighting with a customizable style, plus bookmarks, notes and highlights, are in Readest. — [Readest llms.txt](https://readest.com/llms.txt)
- Word-level read-along is optional in OpenReader (whisper timestamps). Chapter-marked M4B output makes chapter navigation possible. — [noted.lol](https://noted.lol/openreader-webui/); [epub_to_audiobook](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/p0n1/epub_to_audiobook)
- Reading rulers and masking (Helperbird, Read&Write) and colour-gradient line guidance (BeeLine) are offered. The evidence for BeeLine is mixed. — [Helperbird](https://helperbird.com/pricing/); [EdTech Impact](https://edtechimpact.com/products/texthelp); [Leiden](https://universiteitleiden.nl/en/research/research-output/social-and-behavioural-sciences/digital-reading-applications-bring-color-to-reading)
- Regex pronunciation rules (Balabolka) give a precedent for a pronunciation dictionary and skip rules. — [Audeus](https://www.audeus.com/product-reviews/balabolka-alternatives)

### Inferences
Design ideas for a free local study reader, based on the gaps above:
- **PDF cleaning first**: auto-strip repeated headers, footers and page numbers; join hyphens; merge line breaks; offer toggles to skip citations and figure captions; and a preview of the cleaned text before playback. This fixes the root cause of the Edge failures.
- **Bimodal reading**: highlight the current sentence (a word highlight is optional), keep the view centred, and offer optional line focus.
- **Predictability**: no autoplay on open; one consistent default voice; the same keyboard shortcuts everywhere; no pop-ups or upsells.
- **Easy recovery**: "replay last sentence" and "back one paragraph" keys; remember the last position for each file.
- **Chunked progress**: navigate by heading or section, show a progress bar and time remaining per section, and pause automatically at section ends (optional).
- **Pronunciation dictionary**: user-editable, for course jargon such as species names and abbreviations.
- **Local and free**: Kokoro-82M (Apache) or Piper, with no account, no limits and no uploads. This addresses the cost, cap and privacy complaints.
- **Optional export** to MP3/M4B with chapter marks for listening on a commute (audiblez and epub_to_audiobook have shown this works).

### Gaps
- I found no published research or Reddit evidence specifically on autism and TTS preferences (predictability, voice consistency). These ideas are inferred, not sourced.
- Sleep timers and speed caps for ADHD were not researched in product docs.
